Woodmont Trail Counter

A small, low-power sensor that counts how many people use the trails at Woodmont Park, so the Friends of Woodmont Park can show real usage numbers when asking for support.

What it does

  • Counts passes on a trail with a motion (PIR) sensor and reports the total every 10–15 minutes.
  • Counts only. No images, no audio, no personal data.
  • Runs on batteries, with a solar panel planned as a later upgrade.

How it works

 PIR sensor ──Grove──▶ SenseCAP S2110 ──RS485──▶ SenseCAP S2100 ──LoRaWAN──▶ Gateway ──▶ Cloud / dashboard
 (in the woods)        (XIAO RP2040,             (data logger,              (rec center
                        counts + Modbus)          battery powered)           poolhouse)

Hardware

PartRole
SenseCAP S2110 Sensor BuilderXIAO RP2040 board in an IP66 box. Reads the PIR and exposes the count as an RS485/Modbus sensor.
SenseCAP S2100 data loggerPolls the S2110 and sends the count by LoRaWAN. Battery powered.
SenseCAP M2 indoor gateway (US915)Receives uplinks. Planned location: rec center poolhouse, uphill with line of sight to the trail.
Grove PIR motion sensorDetects people passing. To be ordered or confirmed.

Plan

  1. Range test. Put the gateway in the poolhouse and check signal strength from the trail location with the S2100.
  2. Bench prototype. PIR on the S2110, confirm counts show up in SenseCAP Cloud.
  3. Firmware. Debounced counter (ignore re-triggers for ~3 s) exposed as a Modbus register, if the stock firmware doesn't cover it.
  4. Field trial. Battery powered on one trail for a few weeks; compare against a manual count.
  5. Later. Solar power, a second trail, and possibly an on-device camera that reports counts only.

Open questions

  • Does the current S2110 firmware support a PIR out of the box?
  • Best weatherproof mounting height and angle to avoid false triggers from foliage and wildlife.
  • Where to host the data long term (SenseCAP Cloud vs. The Things Network).
  • Park permission and signage before any camera is added.
